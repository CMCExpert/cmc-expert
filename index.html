The Roadmap

Five stages. Most people stall between 3 and 4 — they know how to make the molecule, but not how to defend it to a health authority.

1

Foundation — know the molecule

Molecular biology, strain engineering, fermentation and cell culture fundamentals. You can't develop a process for something you don't understand at the molecular level.

Molecular biologyStrain engineeringFermentation
2

Process Development — make it robustly

Upstream and downstream PD: from clone to GMP-ready process. DOE, scale-up, impurity clearance, and the discipline of proving your process works every time.

Upstream PDDownstream PDQbD / DOEScale-up
3

CMC Breadth — own the whole picture

Drug substance and drug product, analytics, formulation, manufacturing, tech transfer. A CMC expert sees the entire chain, not just their unit operation.

Drug productAnalyticsTech transferPPQ
4

Regulatory Mastery — defend it on paper

ICH guidelines, FDA/EMA expectations, CTD Module 3, comparability, control strategy. The filing is the product, legally speaking — learn to write like a reviewer reads.

ICH Q5–Q14CTD Module 3Comparability
5

CMC Leadership — decide under uncertainty

CMC strategy, health-authority interactions, risk-based decisions with incomplete data, and leading teams through filings. This is the CMC Director's real job.

CMC strategyHA meetingsQRMLeadership

How to use this site

Suggested study loop — 30 minutes a day compounds fast.

Daily

1. Learn

Read one competency domain or one guideline summary in the Knowledge Hub. Take notes on what you'd do differently at work tomorrow.

Daily

2. Test

Run one 10-question round in the Quiz Lab. Wrong answers are the point — each one links to the concept you need to revisit.

Weekly

3. Connect

Map what you learned onto your own projects: where does your current process sit on the roadmap? What would a reviewer ask about it?
