The CMC Director competency map

Six domains. Depth in two or three, working fluency in all six — that's the bar.

01Regulatory & guideline mastery

ICH Q5–Q14, FDA/EMA guidance, CTD structure. Know not just what each guideline says, but why it exists and what a reviewer looks for.

  • CTD Module 3 authoring and review
  • Meeting packages: pre-IND, EOP2, pre-BLA
  • Responding to information requests and CRLs

02Drug substance process development

Upstream and downstream: cell line development through purification, characterization, and scale-up — for mAbs, bispecifics, fusion proteins, and microbial products.

  • Platform vs. molecule-specific development
  • Impurity clearance strategy (HCP, DNA, aggregates, viruses)
  • Process characterization & PPQ

03Drug product & formulation

Formulation development, container closure, stability, and delivery — where the molecule meets the patient.

  • Formulation screening and forced degradation
  • Stability programs (ICH Q5C / Q1A)
  • Device/delivery considerations for biologics

04Analytical & comparability

The methods that prove quality: release, characterization, and the comparability exercises that justify every manufacturing change.

  • Method development, qualification, validation (ICH Q14 / Q2)
  • Comparability per ICH Q5E
  • Reference standards and specifications (Q6B)

05Manufacturing, tech transfer & validation

GMP manufacturing, site transfers, and the three stages of process validation. Paper processes don't treat patients.

  • FDA 2011 process validation: design → PPQ → continued verification
  • Tech transfer and scale-up/scale-down models
  • Deviation, OOS, and change control judgment

06CMC strategy & leadership

The director's real job: integrated CMC plans, risk-based decisions, outsourcing strategy, and leading technical teams through filings.

  • Quality risk management (ICH Q9) applied to CMC decisions
  • CDMO selection, oversight, and governance
  • Lifecycle management (ICH Q12) and PACMPs

Essential guidelines — the short list

If you can explain each of these in two sentences — what it covers and why it matters — you're ahead of most candidates.

GuidelineTopicWhy a CMC expert cares
ICH Q5A(R2)Viral safety evaluation of biotechnology productsDefines how viral clearance is claimed and summed across steps — the basis of every viral-clearance section you'll ever write or defend.
ICH Q5BAnalysis of the expression construct in production cellsWhat you must prove about the genetic construct and its stability in the cell substrate.
ICH Q5CStability testing of biotechnological productsStability protocol design, specs over shelf life — directly shapes your DP strategy.
ICH Q5DCell substrates: derivation and characterisationMCB/WCB requirements — the foundation every upstream process stands on.
ICH Q5EComparability after manufacturing changesThe single most-used guideline in CMC leadership: every process change lives or dies here.
ICH Q6BSpecifications for biotech productsHow acceptance criteria are set and justified — release specs are a negotiation with data.
ICH Q7GMP for active pharmaceutical ingredientsThe GMP rulebook for DS manufacturing, including biologics.
ICH Q8(R2)Pharmaceutical development (QbD)Design space, CQAs, control strategy — the language of modern filings.
ICH Q9Quality risk managementHow to prioritize: risk to the patient decides where CMC effort goes.
ICH Q10Pharmaceutical quality systemThe management system behind every GMP operation you'll oversee.
ICH Q11Development and manufacture of drug substancesLinks development to manufacturing for both chemical and biotech DS.
ICH Q12Product lifecycle managementPost-approval changes, PACMPs — CMC doesn't end at approval.
ICH Q13Continuous manufacturingIncreasingly relevant as continuous DS/DP moves from pilot to filing.
ICH Q14Analytical procedure developmentCompanion to Q2: how methods are developed with the same QbD logic as processes.
ICH M4QCTD — Module 3: QualityThe dossier structure itself. Know where every piece of CMC data lives.
FDA 2011 PVProcess validation: 3 stagesStage 1 design → Stage 2 PPQ → Stage 3 continued verification. The lifecycle model for validation.
Study tip: don't memorize — derive. For each guideline, ask: what patient-safety problem was the industry failing at when this was written? The answer is the guideline's real content.
