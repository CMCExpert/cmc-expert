My Journey so far

From engineering microbes to leading downstream process development — and now building the CMC breadth to become a CMC Director.

A living page. Early-career company names are intentionally left generic — the roles and lessons are what matter. Details get added as the story continues.
Early career · Foundations

Strain engineering & molecular biology

Started where every bioprocess begins: the biology itself. Engineering strains and working with molecular biology tools — cloning, expression systems, host selection. Lesson learned: process problems are usually biology problems in disguise. The engineer who understands the molecule makes better decisions at every scale.

Process development · Microbial

High cell density fermentation — recombinant E. coli

Moved into microbial fermentation: high cell density fed-batch processes for recombinant proteins expressed in E. coli — inclusion body formation, refolding, and the unforgiving economics of volumetric productivity. Lesson learned: in microbial systems, upstream and downstream are one coupled problem — what you grow determines what you must purify.

Process development · Mammalian

Mammalian cell culture — upstream process development

Transitioned to mammalian systems: CHO cell culture, fed-batch and perfusion processes, bioreactor scale-up from bench to pilot and beyond. A different world from E. coli — glycosylation, product quality attributes, and the long timelines of cell culture. Lesson learned: quality can't be tested in at the end; it's designed into the culture.

Now · AGC Biologics (CDMO)

Director / Interim Downstream (DPD) Director

Leading downstream process development: purification platform design, impurity clearance strategy, tech transfer into GMP manufacturing, and PPQ support — while managing a team of 10–12 scientists and engaging VP/CEO-level stakeholders. The CDMO vantage point is unique: you see many molecules, many clients, many failure modes — pattern recognition compounds.

Next

CMC Director — the expert's seat

The goal: own end-to-end CMC strategy — drug substance and drug product, regulatory filings, health-authority interactions, and the judgment calls that decide programs. This website is part of getting there: teaching is the fastest way to find the gaps in your own expertise.

What the journey taught me

Principle 1

Mechanism over memorization

Molecular properties → process choices → analytical methods → optimization. If you can derive the answer from first principles, you don't need to memorize it.

Principle 2

Write like a reviewer reads

The filing is the product, legally. Every experiment should be designed so its write-up survives a health authority's questions.

Principle 3

Decide on arithmetic

Scope, data, and risk — quantified. CMC leadership is making defensible calls with incomplete information, then owning the outcome.
